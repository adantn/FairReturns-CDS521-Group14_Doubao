LLM-generated product attributes

An LLM labels each product’s category, material, fragility and season from its description. Fragile items are returned more than twice as often (4.0% vs 1.7% of training lines), so the labels are model features; their value is measured by the “without product tags” ablation. The LLM labels are checked against keyword rules and against human labels.

Data

Method

  1. Find the return. Each cancellation (“C” invoice) is matched to the customer’s latest earlier purchase of the same product; a line counts as returned if that happens within 60 days.
  2. Describe the order without looking ahead. Customer and product history use only days before the order. Product descriptions are tagged for category, material, fragility and season.
  3. Leave out what the address decides. The country and the postage line (charged by destination) are never inputs. Left in, postage leaks the country, and an adversary even teaches the model to use it in reverse.
  4. Learn a compressed representation. A variational information bottleneck encodes each order into 16 numbers that predict the return.
  5. Make it forget the country that is left. Customer history and order timing still reveal the country. An adversary tries to read the country group from the 16 numbers; gradient reversal trains the encoder to defeat it, given the true outcome (equalized odds).
  6. Check honestly. Time-based test set, counterfactual twins, false-alarm and hit-rate gaps, and a fresh probe classifier that tries to recover the country.

Limitations

  • Returns are inferred from cancellation invoices; same-day cancellations are included and some returns may be matched to the wrong earlier purchase.
  • Country is the customer’s registered country of residence, not nationality. Several groups contain few customers (EIRE: three), so their rates are noisy.
  • The adversary removes some, not all, country information: a fresh probe can still recover part of it from the representation.
  • Equal false-alarm rates and per-country calibration cannot both hold when true return rates differ; we choose the former and report the latter.
  • The pressure to equalise error rates can under-rate individual customers whose orders look typical of one country, even when their own history is risky (invoice 556578 is an example); overall calibration by past return rate stays good.
  • Fairness costs some accuracy: our model’s AUC is a little below the country-aware GBDT’s (see Fairness results).
  • LLM results depend on the model, prompt and date of the run, and on a small audit sample.
